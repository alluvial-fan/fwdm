Choosing where to live in Montreal is hard when you cannot easily tell which areas are a short walk from the things you rely on every day.

This map is for renters and buyers who want to know, before they visit, whether a street corner is a comfortable walk from a metro entrance or a green space.

Pick a walking time for parks, for metros, or for both, and the map colours every street corner by how close it is. The data comes from the STM and the City of Montreal's open data portal.

Disclaimer about the information provided by this map

The map uses the following data:

Metro stops were taken from the STM developer homepage.

Parks were taken from the Montreal City's open data portal.

Neighbourhoods were taken from the Montreal City's open data portal.

Street network (aka geobase) was taken from the Montreal City's open data portal.


On analysis request, coloured dots (representing street corners) will appear to display which areas are within the requested range, and will be coloured based on proximity to said feature(s).

Dot sizes are based on the quantity of streets that connect to said intersection. Smaller side streets will have smaller dots than big 4+way intersections.

The area this map covers is the 19 boroughs of the city of Montreal. The 15 linked municipalities on the island are visually masked out; their corners are measured but not drawn or clickable.

Corners in these linked municipalities are still measured (so walks near the city edge are not cut off), but they are under the grey mask and cannot be selected or interacted with.

Walking calculations are done by calculating distance using the length of streets, corner to corner, and is measured at 1.35m/s (4.9km/h).

Because of this, walking distances should be taken with a grain of salt, as it doesn't account for shortcuts (e.g. walking through a park or public space), or for construction and stop lights.
