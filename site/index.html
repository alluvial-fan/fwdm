While we don't pick where we're born, we later in life get to pick where we'll live; this tool aims to make that process easier.

Type an address or pick a point on the map to see its closest green spaces and metro stations, or set a walking time for either and colour every street corner by how close it is.

    Sources and caveats

    Sources. Metro entrances from the STM; parks, borough boundaries and the street network (Géobase) from the Ville de Montréal open data portal; map tiles from OpenStreetMap. The field's suggestions are searched with Fuse.js (Apache-2.0), vendored in site/vendor. Each file, with its version and its licence, is listed in the README.

    Coverage. All 19 boroughs of the city are drawn; the 15 independent municipalities on the island are greyed out. Their corners are still measured, so a walk near the city edge is not cut short, but they cannot be clicked.

    Walk times. Corner to corner along the street network at 1.35 m/s (4.9 km/h), so they ignore shortcuts through parks, hills, construction and stop lights, and a metro walk ends at an entrance rather than a platform. A dot's size is the number of streets that meet at that corner.

    Address lookup. The same street file is searched in your browser, so a 13 MB file is fetched the first time you look up an address. An ordinal is read the way Montreal writes it, so 5e, 5th and a plain 5 name the same avenue. The names the field offers while you type come from a 108 KB list of them, fetched when you first type rather than with the page. A number or a street the file does not hold is refused, and a refusal offers the nearest names as buttons to take; a misspelling is checked against Photon for its spelling only, and no position it returns is ever used.
